Your attention is not a dataset

Most screen-time trackers are surveillance sold as productivity. They log every app you open and every site you visit, and send it to servers you will never see, to be analysed, profiled or sold.

Organiza is built the other way round. It is open source under the MIT licence. What you do lives in an encrypted database on your device, under a key the system keystore holds. There is no analytics, no tracking and no ad SDK in any build.

Bring your own database: run the backend yourself, or use none at all. Bring your own AI key: the copilot talks to Groq on your account, after names and links are stripped on the device.

You own your dopamine, and you own your data. Read every line of it.

One limit, every device

Android, Linux and Windows run the same core. A two hour limit counts your phone, your laptop and your PC together.

Each device seals its minutes so only your devices can read them; the server sees padded ciphertext.

The Stoic Pause

A blocked app gets no wall to click past. It gets ten seconds of breathing, a question about what you need it for, and a phrase to type word by word.

The app stays frozen behind the pause, its work kept, until you choose.

A copilot on your own key

Paste a free Groq key and the copilot suggests weekly focus blocks and habit tips. Your hardest hours are worked out on the device, with or without it.

The key stays in the keystore, and every prompt passes a sanitizer on the device first, so names and links never leave it.

Habits that level you up

Habits, dailies and to-dos earn experience and keep streaks, in the spirit of a role-playing game.

Thirty day challenges with friends sync only signed, encrypted results: nobody's usage, not even yours.

First, a breath

Ten seconds. A circle grows as you breathe in and shrinks as you breathe out. The urge usually fades within a minute.
